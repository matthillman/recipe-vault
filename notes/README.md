## Conventions

- Prefer small, focused files over long journals.
- Name files descriptively; for experiments, use `YYYY-MM-DD-*.md`.
- If something changes how we cook (technique, timing, temperature, handling), capture it here.
- Prefer distilling source material into notes rather than parking conclusions in `reference/`.

## Folders

- `notes/experiments/`: logs of trials and results.
- `notes/formulas/`: ratios, baker’s % frameworks, conversions, calculators.
- `notes/techniques/`: shaping, fermentation control, scoring, steaming, etc.
- `notes/ingredients/`: flour types, fats, sugars, dairy, etc.
- `notes/equipment/`: pans, stones/steels, mixers, thermometers.
- `notes/modernist/`: hydrocolloids/additives, modernist techniques, and lab-style protocols.
- `notes/science/`: food science notes (extractions, clarification, carbonation/nitro handling, stability).
- `notes/beverages/`: syrups, cocktails, batching, clarification, and service workflows.
- `notes/sources/`: our summaries of external references (link the raw material in `reference/`).

## Writing Pattern

Use a note when you want something more durable than recipe-specific instructions:

- summarize what the technique or finding is
- explain when to use it
- capture measurable targets, risks, or failure modes
- link related recipes, experiments, or source summaries
