# Protocol: Pectinase (Enzymatic) Clarification

## Summary

Pectinase breaks down pectin, which is a major cause of persistent haze in many fruit juices (especially citrus and apple). It pairs well with filtration and/or centrifugation.

This approach is strongly associated with Dave Arnold–style cocktail prep: prevent pectin haze first, then separate solids.

## Safety / Risks

- Enzyme products vary by brand/activity; always follow the manufacturer dosage and handling notes.
- Allergen/processing notes depend on product; record brand/lot used.

## Inputs

- Juice (preferably fresh and strained of coarse pulp)
- Pectinase/pectolase enzyme (brand-specific dosing)

Equipment:

- Thermometer
- Container for warm hold (optional but helpful)
- Filtration setup or centrifuge

## Procedure (General)

1. **Pre-filter:** remove coarse pulp (fine chinois).
2. **Warm (optional):** gently warm juice to a mildly warm range (often ~40–50°C / 104–122°F) if your enzyme benefits from it.
3. **Dose enzyme:** add per manufacturer recommendation; mix gently.
4. **Hold:** rest until viscosity drops and haze begins to settle (commonly 30 minutes to a few hours; log your results).
5. **Separate:** filter (gravity) and/or centrifuge.
6. **Cold hold:** chill and re-check clarity after 24 hours; re-filter if needed.

## Measurements / Targets

- Record:
  - Brand + dose
  - Temp + hold time
  - Clarity after 0h/24h
  - Any aroma loss

## Troubleshooting

- **Haze returns after chilling:** incomplete pectin breakdown or remaining colloids → longer hold, adjust dose, or finer filtration.
- **Weird aromas:** enzyme product or over-processing → confirm product suitability; minimize warm time.
