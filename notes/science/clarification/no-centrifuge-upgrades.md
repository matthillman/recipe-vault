# No-Centrifuge Upgrades (That Actually Move The Needle)

If you don’t have a centrifuge, you can still get “cocktail-clear” with the right combo of **pre-treatment** and **filtration hardware**.

## Highest-Leverage Process Changes

- **Cold crash + settle + decant**: chill overnight, then decant off the sediment before any fine filtration.
- **Treat pectin early**: for many fruit juices, **pectinase + rest** reduces both haze and filter clogging.
- **Stage filtration**: coarse strain → bag/cloth → paper. Skipping a stage is the #1 cause of “coffee filters take forever.”
- **Don’t squeeze**: squeezing cloth/paper forces fines through and re-clouds the liquid.

## Hardware Upgrades (Home-Friendly)

### 1) A Real Bag Filter (Better Than Cheesecloth)

Cheesecloth is inconsistent (weave varies) and tends to pass fines or clog unpredictably.

- Look for a purpose-made fine bag filter (often sold as “nut milk bag” or “Superbag”-style).
- Use it after a mesh/chinois step and before coffee filters.

### 2) Increase Paper Filter Surface Area

Coffee filters work, but the trick is **area** and **parallelization**.

- Use a larger cone (Chemex-style) or multiple funnels at once.
- Pre-wet filters with water to seat them and reduce bypass.
- Stop filtering before the last cloudy tail fraction; re-filter tails separately.

### 3) Vacuum Filtration (Big Quality-of-Life Boost)

Vacuum doesn’t magically clarify more than paper can, but it can make paper filtration **much faster** and more repeatable.

Typical setup:

- Buchner funnel + filter flask
- Vacuum source (vacuum pump or strong shop-vac with a safe trap)
- Filter papers (start coarser, then finer)

Notes:

- Always pre-filter with bag/cloth first or you’ll clog instantly.
- Use food-safe materials; avoid unknown plastics in contact with high-proof alcohol.

### 4) Wine Plate / Pad Filters (Closest Thing To “Production”)

Small home winemaking filter units (plate/pad) can take you beyond coffee filters, especially for batch work.

- Run staged pads (coarse → polishing).
- This works best *after* enzyme treatment and coarse pre-filtration.

## Targets For Nitro / Carbonation

- The goal is **very low particulate** to reduce nucleation, clogging, and unstable head.
- If you see “snow globe” sediment after chilling, you’re not done: settle/decant and re-filter.

## What To Log

- Liquid type (juice/punch), acid/sugar/alcohol (if known)
- Enzyme brand/dose (if used) and hold conditions
- Filtration ladder used (exact order)
- Time-to-filter + yield
- Clarity immediately and after 24 hours cold
