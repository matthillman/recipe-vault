# Protocol: Gel Clarification (Freeze–Thaw)

## Summary

Set the liquid into a gel, freeze it, then let it thaw in a filter. As it melts, clearer liquid drips out while haze-formers stay behind.

This is a general framework that works with agar (and in some cases gelatin), depending on your goals and constraints.

## Safety / Risks

- Food safety: thaw in the fridge when possible; don’t leave perishables at room temp for long periods.

## Inputs

- Base liquid: juice or punch
- Gelling agent:
  - Agar: **0.20–0.35%** (most reliable across acids and alcohol)
  - Gelatin: can work for some applications, but is more temperature-sensitive and can add subtle flavor

Equipment:

- Freezer space
- Filter setup (nut milk bag / Superbag / coffee filter stack)

## Procedure

1. Make and set a gel (see agar activation steps in `notes/science/clarification/agar-clarification.md`).
2. Freeze the fully set gel until solid.
3. Suspend the frozen gel in a filter over a container.
4. Thaw in the refrigerator and let it drip (hours to overnight).
5. Stop when drips become noticeably cloudy (that tail fraction often contains more haze).

## Measurements / Targets

- Track:
  - Starting weight
  - Clarified yield
  - Tail fraction discarded

## Troubleshooting

- **Cloudy from the start:** gel wasn’t strong/activated → increase agar slightly, ensure full activation.
- **Clogs:** too much pulp → pre-filter or settle/decant first.

## Notes

- Freeze–thaw is often slower but can produce very clear results with minimal active handling.
