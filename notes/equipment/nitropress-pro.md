# Equipment Note: NitroPress Pro (Nitro Dispensing)

## Summary

The NitroPress Pro can produce a strong nitro cascade, but it’s much less forgiving than a shaker: **solids, oils, and high viscosity** will clog, sputter, or kill the effect. For cocktails and juices, “clear enough to read through” is the right direction.

## What Works Best

- Very cold liquids (near-fridge or colder) with **low particulate**
- Moderately sweet, not syrup-thick (high brix = slower flow and weaker cascade)
- Low fat/oil (coconut oils and citrus oils are common culprits)

## Prep Checklist (Before Charging)

- Cold crash and decant if possible.
- Stage filtration: mesh/chinois → bag filter → paper polish.
- Avoid squeezing cloth/paper filters.
- If using fruit juice, consider pectinase first (reduces clogging and re-haze risk).

## Serving Workflow (Generic)

Follow the manufacturer’s instructions for fill level, charging, and handling. As a general workflow:

1. Chill the drink and the NitroPress vessel.
2. Fill to the indicated max line (don’t overfill).
3. Charge with nitrogen per the device instructions.
4. Gently invert/roll to mix (avoid aggressive shaking that re-suspends fines).
5. Rest briefly, then dispense immediately.

## Troubleshooting

- **Clogging / sputtering**: particulate or fat/oil → filter finer, skim oils, reduce coconut oil load, treat pectin, discard cloudy tail.
- **Weak cascade**: too warm or too viscous → chill harder; reduce Coco Lopez and replace sweetness/body with clearer components.
- **Excess foam**: over-agitation or too warm → gentler mixing, colder liquid, confirm fill level.

## What To Log

- Temperature at dispense
- Filtration ladder used
- Any additives (enzymes, acids)
- Whether haze returns after 24 hours cold
